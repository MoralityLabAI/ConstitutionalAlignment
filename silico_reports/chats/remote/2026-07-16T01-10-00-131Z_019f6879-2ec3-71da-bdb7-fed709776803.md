## Assistant — 1784164241065

This repo is actually a great mechinterp substrate, for a reason that's rare in interp work: the storyworld gives **exact ground truth for what the model *should* be representing**. The lattice state `a_t` is typed and checkable, reachability labels are computed from exact mechanics, and the membrane gives you an external verifier for every proposal. Usually we probe activations and argue about what a feature "means" — here you can score every internal claim against the lattice.

The "RL loop with extra steps" reading I'd take: **expert iteration**. Train tiny supervised models (TRM-style recurrent proposers, mode heads, routing heads) on frames → roll out → membrane certifies → accepted trajectories become new frames → retrain. That's an RL loop where the reward is replaced by typed certification, and every stage is inspectable. And because these models are tiny (millions of params, not billions), the *full* interp toolbox is affordable: exhaustive activation patching, probes at every step of recurrence, VPD decomposition (you already have `param-decomp` registered in this workspace), multi-seed everything, all cheap on the A100s here.

Concrete experiment directions I see, roughly ordered by how cleanly they test something:

1. **Does `h_t` learn the lattice?** Train a TRM on reachability/deduction frames, then probe whether the private latent linearly encodes candidate-set membership and conflict state. Ground truth is exact, so probe precision/recall is meaningful, and you can measure *when* in the recurrence the lattice representation forms. Causal follow-up: patch the encoded candidate state and check the proposal head follows.

2. **Is soundness typing represented internally?** Probe `h_t` for env-sound vs model-sound vs experience-sound provenance of the current state. If a "soundness direction" exists, steer it and see whether the abstain/conflict head flips — i.e., does the model have an internal analogue of the membrane's provenance rules?

3. **Membrane internalization across the loop.** Run 3–5 expert-iteration rounds under different `MembranePolicy` settings (strict vs `allow_model_sound`) and track how representations drift: does the model learn to self-censor unsound proposals *before* projection (rejection rate falls because proposals get sound, vs. because the model stops exploring)? This is a fully-inspectable toy of how RLHF-style constraints get internalized, and it directly maps the hybrid design space — membrane policy becomes an experimental axis, not a fixed choice.

4. **Mode-head circuit + routing.** The Deduce/Branch/Expand/Explore/Abstain decision on a tiny model is small enough for complete circuit analysis, and VPD can decompose the trained weights into components by frame family — then test the repo's own hypothesis that the right training bridge is *data routing / adapter specialization*, using the components to route.

My recommendation: start with #1 + #2 as one experiment (same trained models, same harvested activations, two probe suites) — it's the cheapest and it establishes the instrument everything else builds on. #3 is the most interesting scientifically but wants the probes from #1/#2 already validated so you can watch representations move across iterations.

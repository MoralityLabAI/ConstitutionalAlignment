<h2>Summary</h2>
<p>Run the base Qwen3.5-9B model under five prompt-only identity frames (plain, constitutional, jinn-accountability, beast/witness, trustee/amanah) against four eval suites, to establish the prompt-effect baseline that every Stage 1 SFT adapter will be compared against. E1 storyworld local-max metrics are scored programmatically in-worker with the existing GPTStoryworld verifier; all other suites are packaged as a blinded bundle for offline judging in the ConstitutionalAlignment TS harness. The frozen identity cards and probe suites produced here are the reusable heldout instruments for the whole R2 program.</p>

<h2>Hypothesis</h2>
<p>Prompt-only identity conditioning produces measurable differences between frames on storyworld local-max quality (E1), pressure-suite behavior (E3), and guardrail responses (E5), relative to the plain and constitutional controls. Support: any frame shifts <code>combined_score</code> or E1 sub-metrics beyond the spread of the plain condition's 3-sample variability. Refute: all five conditions land within that spread — which is itself the useful result, since Stage 1's claim ("SFT effects differ from prompt effects") needs this anchor either way.</p>

<h2>Why this matters</h2>
<p>Without this baseline, any Stage 1 SFT effect is uninterpretable — you can't tell whether the adapter learned a latent policy change or just better prompt-following. Stage 0 is also where the identity cards and eval suites get frozen and hashed; every later comparison in the paper inherits these instruments, so getting them right here is cheaper than re-running four adapters later.</p>
